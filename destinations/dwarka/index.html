About Dwarka

Dwarka is believed to be the kingdom Lord Krishna built after leaving Mathura. The five-storey Dwarkadhish (Jagat Mandir) temple, rising above the Gomti river where it meets the sea, is the western dham of the Char Dham and one of the Sapta Puri, the seven holy cities.

The temple flag, changed several times a day, is visible from far away. Nearby, the Nageshwar Jyotirlinga with its tall Shiva statue and the Rukmini Devi temple are essential stops.

Bet Dwarka, an island said to have been Krishna's residence, can now be reached by road over the Sudarshan Setu, India's longest cable-stayed bridge, opened in 2024.

The town is small and walkable around the temple and the ghats. Evenings are especially atmospheric, when devotees gather on the Gomti ghat for aarti and the lit-up temple is reflected in the water.

Things to do in Dwarka

  1. Dwarkadhish darshan

    Enter via Swarg Dwar after a dip at Gomti ghat (Moksha Dwar is the exit). Mangla aarti in the early morning is special.

  2. Nageshwar Jyotirlinga

    About 17 km from Dwarka on the road to Bet Dwarka; combine both in one half-day trip.

  3. Bet Dwarka

    Cross Sudarshan Setu to the island to visit the Dwarkadhish palace-temple. Boats from Okha are still an option.

  4. Shivrajpur beach

    A Blue Flag-certified clean beach about 12 km away, good for a quiet evening.

  5. Rukmini Devi temple

    About 2 km outside town, with beautiful carvings and a story about Rishi Durvasa.

How to reach Dwarka

By air

Jamnagar (JGA), about 130 km, and Porbandar (PBD), about 100 km, are the nearest airports.

By train

Dwarka (DWK) station is on the Ahmedabad-Okha line, with trains from Ahmedabad, Mumbai, Delhi and Kolkata.

By road

Good highways connect Dwarka with Jamnagar, Porbandar, Somnath (about 230 km) and Ahmedabad (about 440 km).

Getting around

Autos and taxis; shared vehicles for Nageshwar and Bet Dwarka.

Book trains on IRCTC, the official site.

For pilgrims: why it matters and how to go

Char Dham (West) · Sapta Puri · Krishna

Why this place is important

  • Tradition Dwarka is counted among the seven sacred cities (Sapta Puri) in tradition.
  • Tradition Tradition holds that Krishna ruled from Dwarka after leaving Mathura.
  • Historical record Marine archaeology off Dwarka has reported submerged structures. Their dating and meaning are debated by scholars, so treat claims of proof with care.
  • Tradition Nageshwar Jyotirlinga near Dwarka is one claimant among sites in India for this Jyotirlinga; sources differ.

Steps to follow

  1. Reach Dwarka by train (Dwarka station) or road from Jamnagar (about 130 km).
  2. Visit Dwarkadhish (Jagat Mandir) at dawn or in the evening for aarti.
  3. Take the ferry to Bet Dwarka, tradition's residence of Krishna, and plan for waits and weather.
  4. Add Nageshwar Jyotirlinga and Rukmini Devi temple on the same day.
  5. Continue to Somnath (about 230 km) to form a Saurashtra circuit.

Travel tips

  • Combine Dwarka with Somnath for two Jyotirlingas and one dham in a single Saurashtra trip.
  • Carry ID; security checks are thorough.
  • Temple closes in the afternoon - check darshan timings.
Etiquette. Dress modestly; photography is not allowed inside the sanctum.
