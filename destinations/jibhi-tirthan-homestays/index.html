About Jibhi and Tirthan Valley homestays

The Banjar area of Kullu district has become one of Himachal Pradesh's best-loved homestay regions. Jibhi, with its forest, waterfall and wooden houses, and the Tirthan Valley, with its river and access to the Great Himalayan National Park, offer a calmer alternative to Manali and Kasol. Stays are mostly in family-run homes and small guesthouses.

Typical rooms are wooden or stone-and-timber, with views of cedar forest or river. Meals are usually cooked by the hosts, with Himachali dishes such as siddu and local trout on request. Rates commonly range from roughly 800 to 2,500 rupees a room, with higher prices in peak months.

Himachal now requires homestays to register under its Homestay Rules, so ask for the registration number. Road conditions on the way to Jibhi and Tirthan can be tough in monsoon and winter, so confirm access with your host before travelling.

Things to do in Jibhi and Tirthan Valley homestays

  1. Walk to Jibhi waterfall

    A short forest walk from the village. Go early to avoid crowds and take care on wet rocks.

  2. Day trip to Jalori Pass and Serolsar Lake

    A scenic drive and short walk through forest to a quiet lake. The pass can be snowbound in winter.

  3. Great Himalayan National Park treks

    Guided day treks and longer routes begin around Gushaini. Permits and a registered guide are required.

  4. River walks in Tirthan

    Walk along the river and visit small villages. Fishing is regulated; ask your host about rules.

  5. Stay in a family homestay

    Try local Himachali meals and ask your host about village festivals.

How to reach Jibhi and Tirthan Valley homestays

By air

Bhuntar Airport (KUU) near Kullu is the nearest, about 60 to 70 km depending on the village.

By train

No railhead nearby; the closest broad-gauge stations are Chandigarh and Kiratpur Sahib, with long onward road travel.

By road

Overnight buses run from Delhi and Chandigarh to Aut, Banjar and Jibhi. Taxis from Kullu, Bhuntar or Mandi are common.

Getting around

Hosts can arrange local taxis and guides; village roads are narrow.

Book trains on IRCTC, the official site.

Entry tax for vehicles registered outside Himachal

Himachal charges an entry tax at its border barriers for vehicles registered in other states; Himachal-registered vehicles are exempt. A rise to ₹170 for private cars from 1 April 2026 was announced in February 2026, then reportedly rolled back by the Chief Minister on 31 March 2026.

Choosing a homestay here

  • Look for the state registration number on the listing or at the door. Himachal, West Bengal and Ladakh all run official homestay schemes.
  • Ask what the rate includes (meals, hot water, pick-up) and whether rooms have attached bathrooms.
  • Message the host a day before arrival to confirm road access and weather.

Travel tips

  • Ask for the homestay's government registration number before you pay, and check it with the state tourism department.
  • Pay a small advance only through a traceable method (UPI to a named account), never to a personal number you cannot verify.
  • Tell the host your arrival time, food preferences and group size; many homes cook to order and have limited rooms.
  • Carry cash for rural areas: mobile data, UPI and card machines can be patchy.
  • Carry basic medicines and a power bank.
  • Check road status in monsoon and winter before travelling.
Etiquette. Keep noise down in the evenings and avoid littering around streams and forests.
