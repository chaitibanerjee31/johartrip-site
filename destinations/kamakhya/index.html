About Kamakhya (Guwahati)

Kamakhya, on the Nilachal hill overlooking the Brahmaputra, is regarded as one of the most powerful of the Shakti Peethas - the places where parts of Goddess Sati's body fell. There is no idol here; the goddess is worshipped as a natural stone yoni kept moist by an underground spring.

The temple complex includes shrines of the ten Mahavidyas. Its beehive-shaped shikhara is a distinctive example of Assamese temple architecture.

The annual Ambubachi Mela in June, when the temple closes for three days to mark the goddess's annual cycle, draws lakhs of devotees and sadhus from across India.

The temple is a short drive from central Guwahati, which also makes a convenient gateway to Shillong, Cherrapunji and Kaziranga. Most visitors spend half a day at Kamakhya and the rest of their time exploring the riverfront and the city's other temples.

Things to do in Kamakhya

  1. Darshan

    General queues can take hours; a paid special-entry queue is available. Go early in the morning.

  2. Mahavidya temples

    Walk around the hill to the shrines of Bhuvaneshwari, Bagalamukhi, Tara and others.

  3. Umananda temple

    Take a ferry from Uzan Bazar ghat to the Shiva temple on Peacock Island in the Brahmaputra.

  4. Brahmaputra cruise

    Sunset cruises run from Guwahati's riverfront.

  5. Kaziranga National Park

    About 200 km away; jeep safaris to see one-horned rhinos (Nov-April).

How to reach Kamakhya

By air

Lokpriya Gopinath Bordoloi International Airport (GAU) is about 20 km away.

By train

Guwahati (GHY) station is about 8 km away; Kamakhya (KYQ) station is closer.

By road

City buses, taxis and app cabs go up Nilachal hill to the temple.

Getting around

Shared taxis run from Kamakhya gate to the temple.

Book trains on IRCTC, the official site.

For pilgrims: why it matters and how to go

Shakti Peetha · Tantric tradition · Nilachal hill

Why this place is important

  • Scripture The Kalika Purana and Shakta tradition count Kamakhya among the Shakti Peethas, the place where the goddess's yoni is said to have fallen.
  • Tradition The Ambubachi Mela in June marks the goddess's annual menstruation in belief; the temple closes for about three days and reopens with darshan.
  • Tradition The hill temple complex includes ten Mahavidya shrines.

Steps to follow

  1. Reach Guwahati by air or rail, then take a cab or bus up Nilachal Hill (about 8 km).
  2. Pick a queue: free general darshan has long waits; a paid ticket route is offered by the temple board for a shorter queue.
  3. Wear modest dress and carry only what you must; leather items and cameras are restricted inside.
  4. Visit Umananda Island in the Brahmaputra and Navagraha temple on the same trip.

Make the trip count

  • Ambubachi Mela days bring very large crowds and road changes; plan well ahead.
  • Check with the board before paying any private priest for 'quick darshan'.

Travel tips

  • Animal sacrifice still occurs on some days - be prepared.
  • Temple remains closed during Ambubachi's three days.
  • Combine with Shillong (about 100 km) and Cherrapunji.
Etiquette. Follow priests' instructions inside the dark sanctum; photography is not allowed.
