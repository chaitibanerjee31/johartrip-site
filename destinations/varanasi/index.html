About Varanasi (Kashi)

Varanasi, also called Kashi and Banaras, has drawn pilgrims, scholars and seekers for thousands of years. For Hindus it is the city of Lord Shiva, and a visit to the Kashi Vishwanath temple - one of the twelve Jyotirlingas - is the heart of any trip. The rebuilt Kashi Vishwanath Dham corridor now links the temple directly to the river, making darshan followed by a Ganga snan far easier than before.

Life here unfolds on the ghats. At dawn, boats drift past bathers, priests and yoga groups; at dusk, the Ganga Aarti at Dashashwamedh and Assi ghats fills the air with bells, conch shells and lamps. Manikarnika and Harishchandra ghats, where cremations continue day and night, remind visitors why Kashi is believed to grant moksha.

Beyond the temples, Varanasi rewards slow exploration: narrow galis full of sweet shops, Banarasi silk weavers at work, classical music traditions, and Sarnath just outside the city, where the Buddha gave his first sermon.

Highlights

  • Kashi Vishwanath Jyotirlinga and the Vishwanath Dham corridor

Things to do in Varanasi

  1. Darshan at Kashi Vishwanath

    Go early morning or late evening to avoid the longest queues. Mobile phones, bags and leather items are not allowed inside; use the official lockers. Sugam Darshan and aarti tickets can be booked on the temple trust's official website.

  2. Sunrise boat ride

    Hire a rowing or motor boat from Assi or Dashashwamedh ghat around 5:30-6 am and travel the length of the ghats. Agree the fare and duration before boarding.

  3. Ganga Aarti

    The grand aarti at Dashashwamedh starts around sunset. Arrive 45 minutes early for a step seat, or watch from a boat on the river. The morning Subah-e-Banaras aarti at Assi Ghat is quieter.

  4. Walk the old city galis

    Explore Vishwanath Gali, Kachori Gali and Thatheri Bazaar. Try kachori-sabzi, malaiyo (winter only), Banarasi paan and lassi in a kulhad.

  5. Day trip to Sarnath

    About 10 km away, Sarnath has the Dhamek Stupa, the archaeological museum with the original Ashoka Lion Capital, and peaceful monasteries.

  6. Visit other key temples

    Kal Bhairav (the city's guardian), Sankat Mochan Hanuman, Durga Kund and the Annapurna temple near Vishwanath are all part of a traditional Kashi yatra.

How to reach Varanasi

By air

Lal Bahadur Shastri International Airport (VNS) at Babatpur, about 25 km from the city centre, has direct flights from Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Bengaluru, Hyderabad and other cities. Prepaid taxis and app cabs are available.

By train

Varanasi Junction (BSB) and Banaras (BSBS) stations are well connected to all major cities, including Vande Bharat services from Delhi. Pt. Deen Dayal Upadhyaya Junction (DDU), about 17 km away, handles many long-distance trains.

By road

Varanasi is on NH-19. State and private buses run from Prayagraj (about 120 km), Lucknow (about 300 km) and Gaya (about 250 km).

Getting around

Cars cannot enter the old city lanes. Use e-rickshaws and autos to reach the ghats, then walk. Boats are the best way to move between ghats.

Book trains on IRCTC, the official site.

For pilgrims: why it matters and how to go

Sapta Puri · Jyotirlinga (Vishwanath) · Ganga ghats

Why this place is important

  • Scripture The Kashi Khanda of the Skanda Purana praises Kashi as a city dear to Shiva where dying brings liberation (moksha).
  • Scripture Kashi is counted among the seven sacred cities (Sapta Puri) in the Garuda Purana tradition.
  • Tradition Kashi Vishwanath is one of the twelve Jyotirlingas that Shaiva tradition names.
  • Historical record The Kashi Vishwanath Corridor linking the temple to the Ganga was opened in December 2021, which changed how pilgrims reach the temple.
  • Historical record Sarnath, 10 km away, is where the Buddha is recorded to have given his first sermon, so many itineraries pair the two.

Steps to follow

  1. Decide your darshan style first: the general queue is free, and the temple trust advertises a paid 'queue less' darshan on its site.
  2. Reach by train or air (Varanasi Junction, Babatpur airport). Stay near Godowlia or the ghats if you want to walk to the temple; lanes are too narrow for cars.
  3. Dawn boat ride from Assi to Dashashwamedh shows the ghats. Agree the price before boarding.
  4. Evening Ganga Aarti at Dashashwamedh Ghat is free to watch. Arrive 45 minutes early for a standing spot, or hire a boat to watch from the water.
  5. Phones and bags are usually not allowed inside the Vishwanath complex. Use the trust's locker or your hotel.
  6. Do the Panchkroshi or a Sarnath half-day on day two. Keep Manikarnika and Harishchandra ghats for quiet, respectful viewing only; do not photograph cremations.

Make the trip count

  • Hotels fill up in Dev Deepawali (Kartik Purnima) and Mahashivratri. Book a month ahead for those dates.
  • Touts at the station and ghats claim 'special darshan'. Only the temple trust's own booking is genuine.
  • Beware of fixed 'puja packages' priced far above what the trust or a named priest quotes.

Best time to visit

October to March is pleasant. Summers (April-June) are very hot; during monsoon the river rises and some ghats and boat services close.

Month by month: January, February, March, October, November, December.

Travel tips

  • Book stays near Assi, Dashashwamedh or Godowlia to stay walkable to the ghats.
  • Photography of cremations at Manikarnika is considered disrespectful - do not do it.
  • Use only the official temple website for darshan bookings; ignore touts offering 'VIP' entry.
  • Carry small change for boatmen, flowers and offerings.
Etiquette. Dress modestly for temples. Remove footwear before entering ghats-side shrines. Ask before photographing sadhus or rituals.

Frequently asked questions

How many days are enough for Varanasi?

Two full days cover the main temples, a boat ride, aarti and Sarnath. Add a day if you want to explore the old city slowly.

Can non-Hindus visit Kashi Vishwanath?

The temple welcomes devotees; foreign visitors should carry their passport and follow the trust's current rules at the gate.

Which is the best ghat to stay near?

Assi Ghat is calmer with many cafes; Dashashwamedh is central and closest to the main aarti and the temple.
