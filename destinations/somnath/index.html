About Somnath

Somnath is revered as the first of the twelve Jyotirlingas. The temple's history is one of destruction and rebuilding; the present Chalukya-style temple was rebuilt after Independence and completed in 1951. Standing on the shore, it is said that no land lies between it and Antarctica in a straight line south - a fact marked by the Baan Stambh pillar.

The Somnath Trust maintains the temple beautifully. Evening aarti followed by the light-and-sound show on the temple's history, with waves crashing behind, is a highlight.

Nearby, the Triveni Sangam of the Hiran, Kapila and Saraswati rivers and Bhalka Tirth, where Lord Krishna is believed to have left his earthly body, add to the spiritual importance of Prabhas Patan.

Things to do in Somnath

  1. Darshan and aarti

    Aarti is held three times a day. Online puja bookings and guesthouses are available on the Somnath Trust website.

  2. Light-and-sound show

    Held in the evening after the aarti; tickets are sold at the temple.

  3. Triveni Sangam and Geeta Mandir

    Visit the river confluence, Geeta Mandir and the Laxminarayan temple at Dehotsarg.

  4. Bhalka Tirth

    About 5 km away, the place associated with the end of Krishna's earthly life.

  5. Gir National Park

    About 45 km away, home of the Asiatic lion. Safari permits are booked online on the official Gir portal.

How to reach Somnath

By air

Diu Airport is about 85 km away; Rajkot (Hirasar) about 200 km. Keshod airport, about 50 km, has limited flights.

By train

Veraval (VRL), about 7 km, is the main railhead; Somnath station also has some services.

By road

Good roads from Dwarka (about 230 km), Junagadh (about 85 km), Rajkot and Ahmedabad (about 400 km).

Getting around

Autos are easily available.

Book trains on IRCTC, the official site.

For pilgrims: why it matters and how to go

First Jyotirlinga · Saurashtra · Prabhas Patan

Why this place is important

  • Tradition Shaiva tradition lists Somnath first among the twelve Jyotirlingas.
  • Historical record The temple was raided and rebuilt several times over the centuries. The 1025 CE raid by Mahmud of Ghazni is well documented.
  • Historical record The present temple was completed in 1951 and opened with Sardar Patel's backing, according to the trust.
  • Tradition The Triveni Sangam of three rivers and the Bhalka Tirtha, where tradition says Krishna was struck by an arrow, are close by.

Steps to follow

  1. Reach Veraval station (about 7 km) or Diu airport (about 85 km); Rajkot is the nearest large airport.
  2. Attend the morning or evening aarti and the evening light and sound show if it runs.
  3. Book rooms with the trust for dharamshala-level stays, or choose a hotel in Veraval.
  4. Add Bhalka Tirtha, Triveni and Junagadh (about 85 km) with Girnar if you have time.

Frequently asked questions

Why is Somnath called the first Jyotirlinga?

It is listed first in the traditional Dwadasha Jyotirlinga Stotram of twelve Jyotirlingas.
