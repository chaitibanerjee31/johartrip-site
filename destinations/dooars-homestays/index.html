About Dooars homestays

The Dooars is the strip of foothill forest and tea country that stretches across North Bengal toward Bhutan and Assam. It is warm, green and rich in wildlife, with Gorumara and Jaldapara national parks, elephant corridors, rivers and tea estates. Tourism here has grown through community-run and family-run homestays in villages near the forest edge.

A typical stay is a village home or a small cottage cluster with local food, bonfires in winter and guided walks or jeep safaris arranged by the host. Some villages near the Bhutan border, such as Bindu and Jhalong, are known for river views and cardamom and offer a quieter, offbeat feel.

Wildlife sightings cannot be promised, and national park safaris are regulated: book safari slots through the official forest department channels and respect park timings. The Dooars is generally best in the cooler months, as summer is hot and the monsoon closes parts of the parks.

Highlights

  • Gorumara and Jaldapara national parks nearby
  • Tea garden walks and village life
  • River and border-village stays like Jhalong and Bindu
  • Warm winters, good for families
  • Often combined with Darjeeling, Kalimpong or Bhutan

Things to do in Dooars homestays

  1. Jungle safari

    Gorumara and Jaldapara run jeep and elephant-back or vehicle safaris in season. Book through the forest department's official channels and follow guide instructions.

  2. Tea garden walk

    Many estates and villages allow short walks. Ask your host to arrange a visit and a tea tasting.

  3. River and village visits

    Villages along the Jaldhaka river offer walks, picnics and local food.

  4. Birdwatching

    Early mornings around forest edges and tea gardens are excellent, especially in winter.

  5. Visit nearby border villages

    Combine your stay with day trips to Bindu, Jhalong or Samsing, depending on where your homestay is.

How to reach Dooars homestays

By air

Bagdogra Airport (IXB) is the nearest, about 90 to 150 km depending on the village.

By train

New Jalpaiguri (NJP), Alipurduar and Hasimara are the main railheads, with trains from Kolkata, Delhi and Guwahati.

By road

Reserved taxis from NJP, Siliguri or Alipurduar are the usual way; many homestays arrange transfers.

Getting around

Safaris and village tours are arranged by hosts or forest-department guides.

Book trains on IRCTC, the official site.

Choosing a homestay here

  • Look for the state registration number on the listing or at the door. Himachal, West Bengal and Ladakh all run official homestay schemes.
  • Ask what the rate includes (meals, hot water, pick-up) and whether rooms have attached bathrooms.
  • Message the host a day before arrival to confirm road access and weather.

Travel tips

  • Ask for the homestay's government registration number before you pay, and check it with the state tourism department.
  • Pay a small advance only through a traceable method (UPI to a named account), never to a personal number you cannot verify.
  • Tell the host your arrival time, food preferences and group size; many homes cook to order and have limited rooms.
  • Carry cash for rural areas: mobile data, UPI and card machines can be patchy.
  • Wear earthy colours on safari and keep quiet near wildlife.
  • Safari slots in peak months sell out, so book early through official channels.
Etiquette. Never feed wildlife or leave the vehicle during safari, and follow forest-department rules.
