About Hanle and Changthang homestays

Hanle sits at about 4,300 metres in the Changthang region of eastern Ladakh, home to the Indian Astronomical Observatory and some of the darkest skies in the country. The surrounding plateau has Kiang (wild ass) herds, Changpa nomads and lakes such as Tso Moriri and Tso Kar.

Homestays and astro-stays in Hanle and nearby villages are simple, with limited water and electricity, shared toilets and warm meals. Altitude and cold nights make this a demanding destination, and you should be fully acclimatised before you go.

Hanle is a restricted-area destination. Indian citizens need to carry ID and an environment or development fee receipt, and some areas have additional rules. Check the latest rules with Leh administration before you travel.

Highlights

  • Dark-sky stargazing near the Indian Astronomical Observatory
  • Tso Moriri and Tso Kar lakes
  • Changpa nomad culture
  • Kiang wildlife in open plateau
  • Remote village homestays
Health and safety. This is a high-altitude or trekking destination. Consult a doctor before you travel, especially if you are elderly or have heart, lung or blood-pressure conditions. Check weather and official advisories before you set out. Read our disclaimer.

Things to do in Hanle and Changthang homestays

  1. Stargaze at Hanle

    Book a local astro-stay or guided session. Dress very warmly and respect observatory rules.

  2. Visit Hanle Monastery

    A small, remote monastery on a hilltop.

  3. Tso Moriri and Tso Kar

    Combine Hanle with lake visits on the Changthang loop.

  4. Spot Kiang and birds

    Open plateaus support wild ass herds and migratory birds in summer.

  5. Stay with a Changpa family

    Ask whether homestays near Korzok and Hanle can host you.

How to reach Hanle and Changthang homestays

By air

Leh Airport (IXL) is the gateway, followed by a long drive.

By train

There is no railway; the nearest railheads are far away.

By road

Leh to Hanle is about 250 to 270 km via Upshi and Chumathang, taking most of a day; the route from Pangong is also possible in season.

Getting around

Most travellers hire a vehicle with a driver from Leh.

Book trains on IRCTC, the official site.

Inner Line Permit for Indian citizens: no longer required

Ladakh's administration removed the Inner Line Permit requirement for Indian nationals in August 2021. Many older blogs still say it is needed. Indian visitors are instead asked for an environment fee receipt (next rule).

What we could not verify
  • We did not read the original administration notification itself.

Protected Area Permit for foreign nationals

Foreign nationals (including OCI and PIO holders per one source) need a Protected Area Permit for inner-line areas, arranged through a registered Leh travel agent. Some Changthang areas, including Hanle, have extra limits for foreigners.

Cost: ₹600 per person reported by one source.

Reported by several sourcesVehicle rule

Taxis and self-drive cars rented outside Ladakh cannot do sightseeing

Under a Ladakh taxi union rule, a taxi or self-drive car hired outside Ladakh (Delhi, Manali, Srinagar, Chandigarh) may bring you to Leh but cannot be used for sightseeing to Nubra, Pangong, Tso Moriri or Hanle. You hire a Leh-registered taxi for those trips.

How: Book a local Leh taxi from the union rate list for Nubra, Pangong and Tso Moriri circuits.

What we could not verify
  • Sources describe this as a taxi union resolution that is enforced at checkpoints; we found no government notification making it law.
  • Rates change each season; check the union rate card in Leh.

Bikes rented outside Ladakh cannot be used beyond Leh town

A bike rented in Delhi, Manali or Srinagar can enter Leh but riders are turned back from Nubra, Pangong and Hanle circuits. Riders park it and rent a Leh-registered bike (yellow plate) for those days.

Your own private vehicle can be driven within Ladakh

Privately owned cars and bikes (white plate) registered in your own name, or reportedly your spouse's or parent's name, are generally allowed to visit Nubra, Pangong and Tso Moriri. Union members at checkpoints may ask for proof of ownership.

Environment fee receipt and photo ID for restricted-area circuits

Indian tourists pay an environment fee online through the Leh tourist management portal and show the receipt with a photo ID at checkposts for Nubra Valley, Khardung La, Turtuk, Pangong, Tso Moriri and Hanle. The official page confirms that the fee exists and that it includes environment, Red Cross and wildlife components.

Cost: Reported as about ₹450 per person in one 2026 source; another source lists ₹100. The official page we read shows no amount.

How: Pay on the official Leh permit portal (lahdclehpermit.in) or at checkposts. Carry several printed copies of the receipt and ID.

What we could not verify

Choosing a homestay here

  • Look for the state registration number on the listing or at the door. Himachal, West Bengal and Ladakh all run official homestay schemes.
  • Ask what the rate includes (meals, hot water, pick-up) and whether rooms have attached bathrooms.
  • Message the host a day before arrival to confirm road access and weather.

Travel tips

  • Ask for the homestay's government registration number before you pay, and check it with the state tourism department.
  • Pay a small advance only through a traceable method (UPI to a named account), never to a personal number you cannot verify.
  • Tell the host your arrival time, food preferences and group size; many homes cook to order and have limited rooms.
  • Carry cash for rural areas: mobile data, UPI and card machines can be patchy.
  • Acclimatise in Leh for at least two to three days.
  • Carry extra warm clothing; nights are well below freezing even in summer.
Etiquette. Follow observatory rules, keep light pollution low and ask before photographing nomad families.

Frequently asked questions

Can I visit Hanle without a permit?

Indian citizens no longer need an Inner Line Permit, but an environment or development fee receipt and valid ID are checked. Confirm the current rules before you go.
