About Markha Valley trek homestays

The Markha Valley trek is one of Ladakh's most popular routes, typically taking five to eight days. It starts near Chilling or Jingchen and passes through villages like Skiu, Markha, Hankar and Thachungtse before crossing the Kongmaru La pass (about 5,200 m).

Instead of camping, many trekkers use homestays in the valley villages. These are family-run rooms with simple bedding, shared toilets and basic meals. Rates are modest, often around 1,000 to 2,000 rupees per person including dinner and breakfast, though this varies.

The trek requires acclimatisation, fitness and decent gear. Weather can change quickly, and river crossings can be risky after rain. Hire a local guide or go with a registered operator and let someone know your itinerary.

Highlights

  • Village homestays on a classic Himalayan trek
  • Kongmaru La pass crossing
  • Monasteries and barley fields in the valley
  • Views of Kang Yatse and Stok Kangri
  • Direct contact with Ladakhi families
Health and safety. This is a high-altitude or trekking destination. Consult a doctor before you travel, especially if you are elderly or have heart, lung or blood-pressure conditions. Check weather and official advisories before you set out. Read our disclaimer.

Things to do in Markha Valley trek homestays

  1. Trek the full Markha loop

    Plan five to eight days with a guide or operator, depending on your pace and acclimatisation.

  2. Stay in valley villages

    Homestays in Skiu, Markha and Hankar give a warm bed and hot meals along the route.

  3. Cross Kongmaru La

    Start early and go slowly. Weather and altitude need respect.

  4. Visit Hemis Shukpachan or local monasteries

    Short detours add culture to the trek.

  5. Acclimatise in Leh first

    Spend at least two days in Leh before starting.

How to reach Markha Valley trek homestays

By air

Leh Airport (IXL) is the gateway, with flights from Delhi and other cities.

By train

There is no railway to Ladakh; the nearest railheads are far away.

By road

Taxis from Leh take a few hours to the trailhead near Chilling or Shang Sumdo.

Getting around

Trekking agencies and homestay hosts can arrange transfers and guides.

Book trains on IRCTC, the official site.

Taxis and self-drive cars rented outside Ladakh cannot do sightseeing

Under a Ladakh taxi union rule, a taxi or self-drive car hired outside Ladakh (Delhi, Manali, Srinagar, Chandigarh) may bring you to Leh but cannot be used for sightseeing to Nubra, Pangong, Tso Moriri or Hanle. You hire a Leh-registered taxi for those trips.

How: Book a local Leh taxi from the union rate list for Nubra, Pangong and Tso Moriri circuits.

What we could not verify
  • Sources describe this as a taxi union resolution that is enforced at checkpoints; we found no government notification making it law.
  • Rates change each season; check the union rate card in Leh.

Choosing a homestay here

  • Look for the state registration number on the listing or at the door. Himachal, West Bengal and Ladakh all run official homestay schemes.
  • Ask what the rate includes (meals, hot water, pick-up) and whether rooms have attached bathrooms.
  • Message the host a day before arrival to confirm road access and weather.

Travel tips

  • Ask for the homestay's government registration number before you pay, and check it with the state tourism department.
  • Pay a small advance only through a traceable method (UPI to a named account), never to a personal number you cannot verify.
  • Tell the host your arrival time, food preferences and group size; many homes cook to order and have limited rooms.
  • Carry cash for rural areas: mobile data, UPI and card machines can be patchy.
  • Carry a warm sleeping bag; some homestays provide only thin bedding.
  • Check river levels and weather before each stage.
Etiquette. Respect village customs, stay on trails and pack out your waste.
