About Rishikesh

Rishikesh, in the Garhwal foothills, has been a place of meditation for centuries. Today it is known worldwide as the 'Yoga Capital of the World', with hundreds of ashrams and yoga schools on both banks of the clear, fast-flowing Ganga.

The iconic suspension bridges at Ram Jhula and Lakshman Jhula connect temples, ashrams and cafes. The evening Ganga Aarti at Parmarth Niketan and Triveni Ghat is serene and deeply moving.

For adventure lovers, Rishikesh is India's white-water rafting hub, with camps along the river at Shivpuri. A day trip to the Neelkanth Mahadev temple, set in the forested hills, completes a spiritual visit.

The town is divided into busy, temple-filled stretches around the bridges and quieter areas upstream at Tapovan and downstream along the ghats. Most visitors stay two or three days, mixing an aarti, a yoga class and a rafting trip.

Things to do in Rishikesh

  1. Ganga Aarti

    Parmarth Niketan's aarti at sunset includes bhajans and a havan; Triveni Ghat's is more traditional.

  2. Yoga classes

    Drop-in classes run at many ashrams. For longer courses, check that the school is Yoga Alliance or AYUSH-recognised.

  3. River rafting

    Season runs roughly September-June (closed in monsoon). Choose government-registered operators with safety gear.

  4. Neelkanth Mahadev

    About 30 km by road, where Shiva is said to have drunk the poison from the ocean churning.

  5. Beatles Ashram

    The former Maharishi Mahesh Yogi ashram, now inside Rajaji Tiger Reserve, with murals and meditation domes.

How to reach Rishikesh

By air

Jolly Grant Airport, Dehradun (DED), about 20 km away.

By train

Yog Nagari Rishikesh (YNRK) station has direct trains; Haridwar Junction (about 25 km) has many more.

By road

About 240 km from Delhi; buses and taxis are frequent.

Getting around

Shared autos, scooters and walking. Use bridges or boats to cross the river.

Book trains on IRCTC, the official site.

For pilgrims: why it matters and how to go

Yoga capital · Ganga · Char Dham starting point

Why this place is important

  • Tradition Rishikesh is long associated with sages and meditation by the Ganga in tradition.
  • Historical record The Beatles visited Maharishi Mahesh Yogi's ashram here in 1968, which put the town on the world map.
  • Tradition Ram Jhula, Laxman Jhula and the Triveni Ghat aarti are the usual evening sights.
  • Official statement It is a common start for Char Dham road journeys and a base for treks and rafting.

Steps to follow

  1. Reach by train to Haridwar or Rishikesh Yog Nagari station, or Dehradun airport (about 35 km).
  2. Stay in Tapovan or Laxman Jhula for ashram and yoga classes, or near the Ganga for quiet.
  3. Join the Triveni Ghat evening aarti; it is free.
  4. Book rafting only with registered operators and in the official season; rafting is generally not run in the monsoon.
  5. Walk or drive to Neelkanth Mahadev temple (about 32 km by road) if you have a day.

Make the trip count

  • Sale of meat and alcohol is restricted in much of the old town, according to many local guides. Check locally.
  • Pick yoga schools with named teachers and visible certification.

Confirmed on official pageRegistration

Char Dham yatra registration is compulsory (free)

The Uttarakhand portal says you must register yourself and your vehicle before the journey, download a Yatra Registration Letter with a QR code, and verify yourself at each shrine on arrival. It describes registration as free. Helicopter tickets are sold only through IRCTC.

Cost: Free, according to the official page.

How: Register on registrationandtouristcare.uk.gov.in or the Tourist Care Uttarakhand app. Helpline 1364 (toll free) is listed on the page.

What we could not verify
  • Registration date windows and daily caps change each season.
  • Some third-party sites mention fees; the official page says free. Do not pay agents for registration.

Confirmed on official pageVehicle permit

Vehicle registration, Green Card and Trip Card for Char Dham routes

The official transport portal says commercial passenger vehicles (buses, minibuses, taxis) need a Green Card and Trip Card for the Char Dham shrines: ₹400 for up to 10 seats and ₹600 above 10 seats, plus a ₹50 user charge. Private vehicles are asked to register and pay a user charge; no Green Card is mentioned for them.

Cost: Commercial: ₹400 (up to 10 seats) or ₹600 (over 10 seats) plus ₹50 user charge. Private user-charge amount is not stated.

How: Register the vehicle at greencard.uk.gov.in (linked from transport.uk.gov.in).

What we could not verify
  • The private-vehicle user charge amount and Green Card validity are not stated on the page.
  • Some third-party sites say all passenger vehicles including tourists need a Green Card; the official page limits the Green Card to commercial vehicles.

Travel tips

  • Rishikesh is vegetarian and alcohol-free.
  • Do not swim in the Ganga outside safe ghats; currents are deadly.
  • Monkeys are common near the bridges - keep food hidden.
Etiquette. Respect ashram silence rules and dress modestly at ghats.

Is rafting safe in Rishikesh?

Yes with registered operators, helmets and life jackets. Avoid it during monsoon closures.
