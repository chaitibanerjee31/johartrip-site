About Kedarnath

Kedarnath is one of the most sacred and dramatic pilgrimages in India. The ancient stone temple, believed to have been revived by Adi Shankaracharya, stands at about 3,583 metres against the Kedarnath range. It is one of the twelve Jyotirlingas and one of the four shrines of Uttarakhand's Chota Char Dham yatra.

The temple opens only for around six months each year - usually from late April or May (around Akshaya Tritiya) until just after Diwali (Bhai Dooj), when the deity is carried down to Ukhimath for the winter. Exact dates are announced each year by the temple committee.

Reaching the shrine is part of the pilgrimage. The trek of about 16 km from Gaurikund climbs steadily alongside the Mandakini. Ponies, palkis and helicopter services are available for those who cannot walk, and the effort is rewarded by a sunrise view of the temple glowing against the peaks.

Highlights

  • One of the 12 Jyotirlingas and part of the Chota Char Dham
  • Trek of about 16 km from Gaurikund along the Mandakini
  • Samadhi of Adi Shankaracharya behind the temple
  • Bhairavnath temple overlooking the valley
  • Evening aarti with snow peaks behind the shrine
Health and safety. This is a high-altitude or trekking destination. Consult a doctor before you travel, especially if you are elderly or have heart, lung or blood-pressure conditions. Check weather and official advisories before you set out. Read our disclaimer.

Things to do in Kedarnath

  1. Darshan and aarti

    Morning puja and the evening aarti are the most special times. Queues can stretch for hours in peak season (May-June); arriving the evening before and taking darshan at dawn helps.

  2. Trek from Gaurikund

    Start early (by 5-6 am) to reach before afternoon weather changes. Rest stops are available at Jungle Chatti, Bhimbali and Linchauli. Carry rain gear and warm layers even in summer.

  3. Bhairavnath temple

    A short, steep walk of about 1 km above Kedarnath gives the best panoramic view of the temple and the valley.

  4. Shankaracharya Samadhi

    The restored samadhi and statue of Adi Shankaracharya behind the main temple is a quiet place for reflection.

  5. Vasuki Tal (for fit trekkers)

    A high-altitude lake about 8 km beyond Kedarnath. Only attempt with a guide, good weather and proper acclimatisation.

How to reach Kedarnath

By air

Jolly Grant Airport, Dehradun (DED) is the nearest airport, roughly 235-250 km from Gaurikund by road. Helicopter services to Kedarnath operate from Phata, Sersi and Guptkashi; book only through the official IRCTC Heli Yatra portal.

By train

Yog Nagari Rishikesh and Haridwar are the nearest major railheads, around 210-230 km from Gaurikund.

By road

Drive or take a bus from Rishikesh/Haridwar via Devprayag, Rudraprayag and Guptkashi to Sonprayag. Private vehicles stop at Sonprayag; shared jeeps shuttle pilgrims to Gaurikund, the trek start.

Getting around

From Gaurikund: walk, hire a pony/palki at government-fixed rates, or use the helicopter from the valley helipads.

Book trains on IRCTC, the official site.

For pilgrims: why it matters and how to go

Chota Char Dham · Panch Kedar · Jyotirlinga

Why this place is important

  • Tradition Kedarnath is one of the twelve Jyotirlingas in Shaiva tradition.
  • Tradition It is the first of the five Panch Kedar shrines in a Pandava story told in the Himalaya: the Pandavas sought Shiva to atone for the war.
  • Official statement It is one of the four Chota Char Dham sites of Uttarakhand, with Badrinath, Gangotri and Yamunotri.
  • Tradition Adi Shankaracharya is traditionally linked with reviving the temple; this is a tradition, not a dated record.
  • Historical record The June 2013 floods destroyed much of Kedarnath town but left the main temple standing.

Steps to follow

  1. Register first. Uttarakhand states registration is mandatory for yatris and vehicles. Do it on the official portal before you leave, and keep the printout and your ID.
  2. Plan the base: Haridwar or Rishikesh to Sonprayag by road (a long day), then to Gaurikund. Many pilgrims overnight at Guptkashi or Sonprayag.
  3. Choose how to go up: walk about 16 km from Gaurikund, hire a pony or palki, or fly from a helipad near Phata, Sirsi or Guptkashi. Helicopter seats are booked through the official helicopter booking portal.
  4. Start the walk before dawn, carry rain gear, a light jacket, ORS and snacks. Nights above 3,500 m are cold even in summer.
  5. Stay at Kedarnath overnight if you can: evening aarti and the dawn queue are quieter than the midday rush.
  6. Check weather and road status the evening before each leg. Landslides close the Gaurikund and Sonprayag roads often in monsoon.

Make the trip count

  • The temple is open only for part of the year, roughly late April or May to October or November, with dates set each year. Confirm opening dates.
  • If you have heart, lung or blood pressure conditions, see a doctor before the climb. Do not rely on ponies if you are unwell.
  • Do not book helicopters through random WhatsApp agents. Use the official portal.

Confirmed on official pageRegistration

Char Dham yatra registration is compulsory (free)

The Uttarakhand portal says you must register yourself and your vehicle before the journey, download a Yatra Registration Letter with a QR code, and verify yourself at each shrine on arrival. It describes registration as free. Helicopter tickets are sold only through IRCTC.

Cost: Free, according to the official page.

How: Register on registrationandtouristcare.uk.gov.in or the Tourist Care Uttarakhand app. Helpline 1364 (toll free) is listed on the page.

What we could not verify
  • Registration date windows and daily caps change each season.
  • Some third-party sites mention fees; the official page says free. Do not pay agents for registration.

Confirmed on official pageVehicle permit

Vehicle registration, Green Card and Trip Card for Char Dham routes

The official transport portal says commercial passenger vehicles (buses, minibuses, taxis) need a Green Card and Trip Card for the Char Dham shrines: ₹400 for up to 10 seats and ₹600 above 10 seats, plus a ₹50 user charge. Private vehicles are asked to register and pay a user charge; no Green Card is mentioned for them.

Cost: Commercial: ₹400 (up to 10 seats) or ₹600 (over 10 seats) plus ₹50 user charge. Private user-charge amount is not stated.

How: Register the vehicle at greencard.uk.gov.in (linked from transport.uk.gov.in).

What we could not verify
  • The private-vehicle user charge amount and Green Card validity are not stated on the page.
  • Some third-party sites say all passenger vehicles including tourists need a Green Card; the official page limits the Green Card to commercial vehicles.

Best time to visit

May-June and mid-September to October. Avoid July-August monsoon, when landslides frequently block roads. The temple is closed from November to April.

Month by month: May, June, September, October.

Festivals

Kapat opening ceremony (around Akshaya Tritiya)Shravan monthKapat closing on Bhai Dooj

Travel tips

  • Registration on the Uttarakhand Tourism Char Dham portal is mandatory for all pilgrims - do it before you travel.
  • Acclimatise: spend a night at Guptkashi or Sonprayag before the trek.
  • Carry a basic medical kit, ORS, rain poncho, torch and warm jacket.
  • People with heart or breathing conditions should consult a doctor before going.
  • Book helicopter tickets only on the official portal - fake websites are common.
Etiquette. The shrine is at high altitude and very crowded; be patient in queues and keep the trail clean - carry your waste back down.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the Kedarnath trek?

About 16 km one way from Gaurikund. Most walkers take 6-9 hours to go up.

Is registration compulsory?

Yes. Uttarakhand requires registration for the Char Dham yatra; check the official tourism portal for the current process.

When does Kedarnath open?

Usually late April or May, closing around Bhai Dooj in October-November. Dates are announced every year.
