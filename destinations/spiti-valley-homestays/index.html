About Spiti Valley homestays

Spiti is a high-altitude cold desert in Himachal Pradesh, with villages at roughly 3,500 to 4,500 metres and monasteries such as Key, Tabo and Dhankar. Most villages have traditional mud-brick homes where families host guests, with shared dining, warm kitchens and simple rooms.

Homestays here are often the only option outside Kaza. Expect shared or basic toilets, limited water in winter and heating by stove. Meals are generally included and focus on local barley, dal, rice and butter tea. Rates commonly range from about 800 to 2,000 rupees per person with meals, higher at peak times.

Altitude is the main health risk. Acclimatise gently, avoid alcohol on arrival and know the symptoms of altitude sickness. Roads may close due to snow or landslides; the Manali route via Kunzum La is usually closed in winter, while the Shimla–Kinnaur route stays more reliable.

Highlights

  • Mud-brick homestays in high villages
  • Key, Tabo and Dhankar monasteries
  • Clear night skies for stargazing
  • Villages like Langza, Komic and Hikkim
  • Cold-desert landscapes and fossil sites
Health and safety. This is a high-altitude or trekking destination. Consult a doctor before you travel, especially if you are elderly or have heart, lung or blood-pressure conditions. Check weather and official advisories before you set out. Read our disclaimer.

Things to do in Spiti Valley homestays

  1. Visit Key Monastery

    A large hilltop monastery near Kaza. Respect prayer times and dress modestly.

  2. Stay in a village homestay

    Kibber, Langza and Komic offer family stays; book ahead for peak season.

  3. Stargaze

    Spiti has very dark skies. Dress warmly and ask your host for the best spots.

  4. Tabo and Dhankar

    Ancient monasteries with remarkable murals; check opening hours and photography rules.

  5. Acclimatise first

    Spend a night at moderate height before going higher, and avoid rushing.

How to reach Spiti Valley homestays

By air

Bhuntar Airport (KUU) near Kullu is the nearest, but it is a long drive onward.

By train

No railhead; Shimla and Chandigarh are the usual rail connections, followed by long road travel.

By road

From Shimla via Kinnaur to Kaza is about two days; from Manali via Atal Tunnel and Kunzum La is shorter but usually open only in summer.

Getting around

Shared taxis connect villages; many travellers hire a vehicle with a driver.

Book trains on IRCTC, the official site.

Himachal homestays must be registered (Homestay Rules, 2025)

Himachal Pradesh has made registration of homestays and B&Bs mandatory under its Homestay Rules, 2025, with an online portal launched in February 2026. Guests can ask the host for the registration number.

Foreign nationals need a permit beyond Jangi to Spiti

Foreign nationals need an Inner Line or Protected Area Permit for the border belt between Jangi in Kinnaur and Spiti (Kaza, Tabo, Dhankar, Key, Kibber, Langza, Komic, Hikkim). Kalpa, Sangla, Chitkul and Sarahan are reported as permit-free.

Cost: Reported as free and same-day at Reckong Peo in one source.

How: Apply at the district offices on the route (Reckong Peo, Kaza, Keylong, Shimla). Shimla has required a group of two foreigners in one source.

What we could not verify
  • Fee and group rules vary between sources; confirm with the SDM office before travelling.

Rohtang Pass vehicle permit (only if you cross the pass from Manali)

Vehicles going over Rohtang Pass from the Manali side need an online day permit, tied to one vehicle and date, issued against a daily quota. The Atal Tunnel route to Lahaul and Spiti does not need it. The pass is closed on Tuesdays and each vehicle is limited to a few permits per week.

Cost: Not confirmed. Sources quote different figures.

How: Apply online with RC, PUC and photo ID; carry a printout.

Entry tax for vehicles registered outside Himachal

Himachal charges an entry tax at its border barriers for vehicles registered in other states; Himachal-registered vehicles are exempt. A rise to ₹170 for private cars from 1 April 2026 was announced in February 2026, then reportedly rolled back by the Chief Minister on 31 March 2026.

Cost: Cars, jeeps and vans: ₹100 reported in June 2026; 6 to 12 seat vehicles ₹130. Earlier reports said ₹170. Two-wheeler rate not found.

Choosing a homestay here

  • Look for the state registration number on the listing or at the door. Himachal, West Bengal and Ladakh all run official homestay schemes.
  • Ask what the rate includes (meals, hot water, pick-up) and whether rooms have attached bathrooms.
  • Message the host a day before arrival to confirm road access and weather.

Travel tips

  • Ask for the homestay's government registration number before you pay, and check it with the state tourism department.
  • Pay a small advance only through a traceable method (UPI to a named account), never to a personal number you cannot verify.
  • Tell the host your arrival time, food preferences and group size; many homes cook to order and have limited rooms.
  • Carry cash for rural areas: mobile data, UPI and card machines can be patchy.
  • Acclimatise before ascending; carry basic medicine and a thermos.
  • Fuel and cash are limited; fill up and withdraw cash in advance.
Etiquette. Walk clockwise around stupas and monasteries and ask before taking photos of monks or locals.
