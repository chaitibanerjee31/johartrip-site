About Goa

Goa's coastline stretches for over 100 km, from busy northern beaches like Baga and Calangute to quieter southern ones like Palolem and Agonda. Its 450 years of Portuguese history left colourful villages, Latin-quarter streets in Panaji and the UNESCO-listed churches of Old Goa.

Inland, Goa has an important Hindu heritage. The Mangueshi and Shantadurga temples near Ponda, with their distinctive lamp towers (deepastambhas), are among the most visited.

Spice plantations, Dudhsagar waterfalls, river cruises and Goan cuisine - fish curry-rice, xacuti and bebinca - round out the experience.

Goa is small enough to see a lot in a few days, but each region has its own character. North Goa is lively and crowded in season, central Goa around Panaji has heritage and good food, and South Goa is slower, with long clean beaches and family-friendly stays. A scooter or a hired car gives the most freedom.

Things to do in Goa

  1. Beach hopping

    North for buzz (Anjuna, Vagator), South for calm (Palolem, Agonda, Cola).

  2. Old Goa churches

    See the Basilica of Bom Jesus and Se Cathedral.

  3. Ponda temple circuit

    Visit Mangueshi, Shantadurga and Mahalasa temples in a half-day.

  4. Dudhsagar Falls

    Best just after the monsoon (Oct-Nov); jeep safaris run from Kulem.

  5. Spice farm tour

    Tours near Ponda include a traditional Goan lunch.

How to reach Goa

By air

Two airports: Dabolim (GOI) in the south-centre and Manohar International (GOX) at Mopa in the north.

By train

Madgaon (MAO), Thivim (THVM) and Karmali (KRMI) on the Konkan Railway.

By road

About 590 km from Mumbai and 560 km from Bengaluru; overnight buses are common.

Getting around

Rent a scooter or car; app cabs are limited, local taxis and GoaMiles operate.

Book trains on IRCTC, the official site.

Travel tips

  • Wear helmets on scooters; police checks are frequent.
  • Swim only in lifeguard zones.
  • Book December stays early.
