About Puri

Puri is the eastern dham of the Char Dham yatra. The 12th-century Shree Jagannath temple, with its towering shikhara and flag that is changed every day, is dedicated to Lord Jagannath with his siblings Balabhadra and Subhadra. Entry inside the temple is restricted to Hindus.

Puri is best known for the Rath Yatra, held on Ashadha Shukla Dwitiya (June-July), when the three deities ride enormous wooden chariots along the Bada Danda to the Gundicha temple, pulled by lakhs of devotees. Mahaprasad, cooked in earthen pots in what is said to be the world's largest temple kitchen, is sold at the Ananda Bazar inside the complex.

Puri also makes a perfect base for Odisha's Golden Triangle: the Sun Temple at Konark, the temples of Bhubaneswar, and the lagoon at Chilika.

Things to do in Puri

  1. Jagannath darshan

    Mobile phones, cameras and leather items are not allowed; deposit them at the counters outside. Mornings are calmer. Beware of pandas demanding large fees - you are not obliged to hire one.

  2. Eat Mahaprasad

    Buy the full mahaprasad (rice, dal, vegetables, sweets) at Ananda Bazar inside the temple complex.

  3. Konark Sun Temple

    About 35 km along the Marine Drive. The 13th-century chariot-shaped temple is a UNESCO site; go early morning.

  4. Puri beach

    Swim only in flagged zones with lifeguards; currents are strong. Visit Sudarsan Pattnaik's sand art in the evening.

  5. Chilika Lake

    From Satapada (about 50 km), take a boat to spot Irrawaddy dolphins; in winter, Mangalajodi is great for migratory birds.

  6. Raghurajpur heritage village

    About 14 km away; watch artists make Pattachitra paintings.

How to reach Puri

By air

Biju Patnaik International Airport, Bhubaneswar (BBI), about 60 km away.

By train

Puri (PURI) station has direct trains from Kolkata (Howrah), Delhi, Chennai, Mumbai and other cities.

By road

Well-connected to Bhubaneswar (about 60 km) by NH-316; frequent buses and taxis.

Getting around

Autos, e-rickshaws and cycle-rickshaws; vehicles stop away from the temple on big days.

Book trains on IRCTC, the official site.

For pilgrims: why it matters and how to go

Char Dham (East) · Jagannath · Rath Yatra

Why this place is important

  • Tradition Puri is one of the four Char Dham sites placed at the four corners of India in Shankaracharya tradition.
  • Tradition The Rath Yatra, when Jagannath, Balabhadra and Subhadra are drawn on chariots, is held in the month of Ashadha. It is among the best-known processions in India.
  • Tradition The temple kitchen's mahaprasad is eaten by sitting together irrespective of caste, according to temple custom; it is sold at Ananda Bazaar.
  • Official statement Konark Sun Temple, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is about 35 km away and is often paired with Puri.

Steps to follow

  1. Check the entry rule first: the main Jagannath temple admits Hindus only, and the temple administration sets the rules. Others can see the temple from Raghunandan Library rooftop, according to common local advice.
  2. Reach Puri by train from Bhubaneswar (about 60 km) or road.
  3. Visit in the morning, remove leather and bags, store phones at the counters. Rules on photography are strict.
  4. Buy mahaprasad at Ananda Bazaar, not from outside vendors.
  5. Add Chilika Lake, Konark and Pipili as day trips.

Make the trip count

  • Rath Yatra week sees very large crowds. Book stays months ahead and expect closures near the Grand Road.
  • Pandas (priests) may approach you. Agree any ritual and its price in writing before it starts.
  • Sea currents at Puri beach are strong; swim only at flagged zones with lifeguards.

Travel tips

  • Book rooms 3-6 months ahead for Rath Yatra.
  • Non-Hindus can view the temple from the Raghunandan Library roof opposite (small donation).
  • Carry minimal valuables to the temple.
  • Avoid the sea during rough weather and the monsoon.
Etiquette. Wear modest traditional clothes. Follow temple rules on photography strictly.

Frequently asked questions

When is Rath Yatra?

On Ashadha Shukla Dwitiya, usually in June or July.

Can foreigners enter the Jagannath temple?

No, entry is restricted to Hindus; others can view it from nearby rooftops.
