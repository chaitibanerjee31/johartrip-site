About Kaziranga National Park

Kaziranga stretches along the Brahmaputra, with tall elephant grass, wetlands and woodlands. It protects the Indian one-horned rhino, tigers, wild buffalo, swamp deer, elephants and many migratory birds.

Jeep safaris run in four main zones. Central (Kohora) is the most popular for rhinos, Western (Bagori) and Eastern (Agoratoli) are good for birds, and Burapahar has a quieter, forest feel. Book safaris through the official forest department channels.

The park is closed through the monsoon, when the Brahmaputra floods. Winter mornings can be foggy and cold, but this is the best time for sightings.

Highlights

  • Central Range (Kohora) jeep safari
  • Bagori Western Range
  • Agoratoli Eastern Range
  • Tea estates of Jorhat
  • Majuli river island

Things to do in Kaziranga National Park

  1. Morning jeep safari

    Early slots are cooler and more active. Book ahead.

  2. Birdwatching

    Winter migrants arrive. Try Bagori and Agoratoli.

  3. Stay at a lodge

    Lodges around Kohora offer cultural evenings.

  4. Tea estate visit

    Visit tea gardens in the region.

  5. Add Majuli

    The river island has Vaishnav monasteries and mask-making.

How to reach Kaziranga National Park

By air

Jorhat (about 95 km) and Guwahati (about 220 km) are the nearest airports.

By train

Furkating Junction (about 75 km) is the nearest major railhead.

By road

Kohora is on the national highway, about 220 km from Guwahati.

Getting around

Safari jeeps are booked through the park office or lodges.

Book trains on IRCTC, the official site.

Travel tips

  • Book safaris in advance for December and January.
  • Carry layers; morning safaris are cold.
  • Sightings are never guaranteed.
Etiquette. Keep quiet and stay seated in the jeep; never leave it.
