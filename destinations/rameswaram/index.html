About Rameswaram

Rameswaram is where, according to the Ramayana, Lord Rama worshipped Shiva before building the bridge to Lanka. The Ramanathaswamy temple houses a Jyotirlinga and is the southern dham of the Char Dham, making it one of the holiest places in India.

Pilgrims traditionally bathe first at the Agni Theertham in the sea, then at the 22 theerthams (holy wells) inside the temple, before darshan. The temple's pillared corridors - over a kilometre in total - are among the longest in the world.

The island is linked to the mainland by the Pamban road bridge and the new vertical-lift Pamban railway bridge. At its tip lies Dhanushkodi, destroyed by a cyclone in 1964, where the Bay of Bengal meets the Indian Ocean.

Things to do in Rameswaram

  1. Theertham snan and darshan

    Bathe at Agni Theertham, then follow a temple guide through the 22 wells. Carry a change of clothes; lockers and changing rooms are near the temple.

  2. Dhanushkodi

    About 20 km away. Drive to Arichal Munai, the land's end, and see the ruins of the old church and railway station. Leave before sunset.

  3. APJ Abdul Kalam Memorial

    A beautiful memorial to the former President, who was born in Rameswaram.

  4. Panchmukhi Hanuman and Ram Jharokha

    See floating stones at the Panchmukhi Hanuman temple and the island view from Gandhamadhana Parvatham.

  5. Pamban bridge

    Stop on the road bridge for views of the sea and the railway bridge.

How to reach Rameswaram

By air

Madurai Airport (IXM) is about 175 km away; Tuticorin is another option.

By train

Rameswaram (RMM) station is connected via the Pamban rail bridge; trains run from Chennai, Madurai and other cities. Check current schedules on IRCTC.

By road

About 175 km from Madurai via NH-87; government and private buses run regularly.

Getting around

Autos are the easiest way around the island; jeeps/buses run to Dhanushkodi.

Book trains on IRCTC, the official site.

For pilgrims: why it matters and how to go

Char Dham (South) · Jyotirlinga · Ramayana tradition

Why this place is important

  • Tradition Ramayana tradition holds that Rama worshipped Shiva here before crossing to Lanka.
  • Tradition Ramanathaswamy is counted among the twelve Jyotirlingas in Shaiva tradition.
  • Tradition It is one of the four Char Dham sites in the Shankaracharya scheme.
  • Tradition Pilgrims bathe in 22 theerthams (sacred wells) inside the temple complex, a custom tied to the temple.
  • Historical record Dhanushkodi, the ruined town at the end of the road, was destroyed by a cyclone in 1964.

Steps to follow

  1. Do Agni Theertham (sea) first, then the 22 theerthams inside the temple. Set a clear price with a guide beforehand or use the temple's own counters.
  2. Wear dry clothes after the bath; most pilgrims change at lodges and carry a spare set.
  3. Visit the Ramanathaswamy temple's long pillared corridors in the morning, when it is cooler.
  4. Add Dhanushkodi, Pamban bridge and Gandamadana Parvatham as half-day stops.
  5. Combine with Madurai (about 170 km) for Meenakshi temple.

Travel tips

  • Wear traditional clothes; trousers/jeans may not be allowed in the sanctum.

Etiquette. Wet clothes are fine during the theertham circuit but change before darshan as instructed.

Frequently asked questions

Why bathe in 22 wells?

Each theertham is believed to purify a different aspect of the self; the bath is part of the traditional ritual before darshan.
