About Kinnaur homestays: Sangla, Chitkul and Kalpa

Kinnaur lies along the old Hindustan–Tibet road and is known for apple orchards, wooden temples and snow peaks. Kalpa offers views of Kinner Kailash, Sangla lies in the Baspa valley, and Chitkul is among the last villages before the border. Homestays are common in all three.

Rooms are typically simple and warm, with meals served by the family. Rates commonly range between roughly 1,000 and 2,500 rupees a room, higher in peak seasons. Spring and early autumn are the most pleasant; winter brings snow and closes some roads.

Kinnaur also has religious significance, with temples such as Kamru Fort and the Kinner Kailash peak, where an annual yatra is held. Respect local customs and be aware that some areas near the border have restrictions on photography.

Highlights

  • Apple orchards in Sangla and Kalpa
  • Views of Kinner Kailash range
  • Chitkul, one of the last villages near the border
  • Wooden temples and Kinnauri architecture
  • Autumn colours and spring blossoms
Health and safety. This is a high-altitude or trekking destination. Consult a doctor before you travel, especially if you are elderly or have heart, lung or blood-pressure conditions. Check weather and official advisories before you set out. Read our disclaimer.

Things to do in Kinnaur homestays: Sangla, Chitkul and Kalpa

  1. Walk through Chitkul

    A small village with old houses and a temple. Respect local rules on photography and restricted zones.

  2. Sangla valley walk

    Walk along the Baspa river and through orchards.

  3. See Kinner Kailash from Kalpa

    On clear days, the peak glows at sunrise and sunset.

  4. Visit Kamru Fort

    A wooden fort and temple in Sangla. Dress modestly and follow temple rules.

  5. Stay in a local homestay

    Try local apples, rajma and Kinnauri dishes.

How to reach Kinnaur homestays: Sangla, Chitkul and Kalpa

By air

Shimla Airport is nearest but has limited flights; Chandigarh is the practical hub.

By train

Shimla (via narrow-gauge) and Kalka are the nearest stations.

By road

Buses and taxis run from Shimla to Rekong Peo, Kalpa, Sangla and Chitkul along NH5 and link roads.

Getting around

Local taxis connect villages; plan for slow, winding roads.

Book trains on IRCTC, the official site.

Himachal homestays must be registered (Homestay Rules, 2025)

Himachal Pradesh has made registration of homestays and B&Bs mandatory under its Homestay Rules, 2025, with an online portal launched in February 2026. Guests can ask the host for the registration number.

How: Hosts register at homestay.hp.gov.in. Guests: ask for the number before paying.

Indian citizens: no permit for the Shimla-Kinnaur-Spiti route; carry photo ID

Most 2026 guides agree Indian citizens need no Inner Line Permit for Kinnaur and Spiti, only a government photo ID for checkpost registration (for example at Jangi).

Foreign nationals need a permit beyond Jangi to Spiti

Foreign nationals need an Inner Line or Protected Area Permit for the border belt between Jangi in Kinnaur and Spiti (Kaza, Tabo, Dhankar, Key, Kibber, Langza, Komic, Hikkim). Kalpa, Sangla, Chitkul and Sarahan are reported as permit-free.

Cost: Reported as free and same-day at Reckong Peo in one source.

How: Apply at the district offices on the route (Reckong Peo, Kaza, Keylong, Shimla). Shimla has required a group of two foreigners in one source.

What we could not verify
  • Fee and group rules vary between sources; confirm with the SDM office before travelling.

Entry tax for vehicles registered outside Himachal

Himachal charges an entry tax at its border barriers for vehicles registered in other states; Himachal-registered vehicles are exempt. A rise to ₹170 for private cars from 1 April 2026 was announced in February 2026, then reportedly rolled back by the Chief Minister on 31 March 2026.

Cost: Cars, jeeps and vans: ₹100 reported in June 2026; 6 to 12 seat vehicles ₹130. Earlier reports said ₹170. Two-wheeler rate not found.

Choosing a homestay here

  • Look for the state registration number on the listing or at the door. Himachal, West Bengal and Ladakh all run official homestay schemes.
  • Ask what the rate includes (meals, hot water, pick-up) and whether rooms have attached bathrooms.
  • Message the host a day before arrival to confirm road access and weather.

Travel tips

  • Ask for the homestay's government registration number before you pay, and check it with the state tourism department.
  • Pay a small advance only through a traceable method (UPI to a named account), never to a personal number you cannot verify.
  • Tell the host your arrival time, food preferences and group size; many homes cook to order and have limited rooms.
  • Carry cash for rural areas: mobile data, UPI and card machines can be patchy.
  • Roads are narrow with landslide risk; allow extra time.
  • Photography near the border may be restricted.
Etiquette. Dress modestly at temples and ask before photographing people or sacred sites.
