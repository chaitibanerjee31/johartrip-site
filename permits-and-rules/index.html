Permits, fees and vehicle rules

For people and vehicles: Inner Line Permits, environment fees, entry taxes, vehicle registration and local transport rules. Every item shows how sure we are.

We list a rule as confirmed only when we read it on the official page. Where sources disagree or we found nothing reliable, we say so. Coverage is limited to the places below; other guides say "not yet researched". How we verify.

Himachal Pradesh

Reported by several sourcesRegistration

Himachal homestays must be registered (Homestay Rules, 2025)

Himachal Pradesh has made registration of homestays and B&Bs mandatory under its Homestay Rules, 2025, with an online portal launched in February 2026. Guests can ask the host for the registration number.

How: Hosts register at homestay.hp.gov.in. Guests: ask for the number before paying.

What we could not verify
  • Our checker is blocked by the portal's robots.txt, so we could not read the official page itself.
  • We found no public tool for guests to verify a registration number.

Indian citizens: no permit for the Shimla-Kinnaur-Spiti route; carry photo ID

Most 2026 guides agree Indian citizens need no Inner Line Permit for Kinnaur and Spiti, only a government photo ID for checkpost registration (for example at Jangi).

What we could not verify
  • One local website says Indians need a permit for Chitkul beyond Sangla and for Nako and Hangrang. It disagrees with at least eight other sources, so confirm at the Reckong Peo SDM office if you plan those villages.
  • We did not read an official Himachal notification.

Foreign nationals need a permit beyond Jangi to Spiti

Foreign nationals need an Inner Line or Protected Area Permit for the border belt between Jangi in Kinnaur and Spiti (Kaza, Tabo, Dhankar, Key, Kibber, Langza, Komic, Hikkim). Kalpa, Sangla, Chitkul and Sarahan are reported as permit-free.

Cost: Reported as free and same-day at Reckong Peo in one source.

How: Apply at the district offices on the route (Reckong Peo, Kaza, Keylong, Shimla). Shimla has required a group of two foreigners in one source.

What we could not verify
  • Fee and group rules vary between sources; confirm with the SDM office before travelling.

Rohtang Pass vehicle permit (only if you cross the pass from Manali)

Vehicles going over Rohtang Pass from the Manali side need an online day permit, tied to one vehicle and date, issued against a daily quota. The Atal Tunnel route to Lahaul and Spiti does not need it. The pass is closed on Tuesdays and each vehicle is limited to a few permits per week.

Cost: Not confirmed. Sources quote different figures.

How: Apply online with RC, PUC and photo ID; carry a printout.

What we could not verify
  • Fee amount is not confirmed.
  • Sources give two portal addresses (rohtangpermits.nic.in and rohtangpermits.hp.gov.in); use the one linked from the Kullu district site.
  • Our checker is blocked by the portal's robots.txt, so we could not read it.
  • Opening dates depend on snow clearance each year.

Entry tax for vehicles registered outside Himachal

Himachal charges an entry tax at its border barriers for vehicles registered in other states; Himachal-registered vehicles are exempt. A rise to ₹170 for private cars from 1 April 2026 was announced in February 2026, then reportedly rolled back by the Chief Minister on 31 March 2026.

Cost: Cars, jeeps and vans: ₹100 reported in June 2026; 6 to 12 seat vehicles ₹130. Earlier reports said ₹170. Two-wheeler rate not found.

What we could not verify
  • Reports from February and June 2026 disagree on the current car rate.
  • Two-wheeler and taxi rates were not found.
  • FASTag-based collection was announced; whether it is live is unknown.

Development fee barrier before Kasol and Manikaran

A local authority barrier about 2 km before Kasol charges a development fee. The only rates we found date from 2021 (bike ₹50, car ₹100, SUV or MUV ₹300), and a May 2026 report says Himachal vehicles are now charged too.

Ladakh

Reported by several sourcesPerson permit · Indian citizens

Inner Line Permit for Indian citizens: no longer required

Ladakh's administration removed the Inner Line Permit requirement for Indian nationals in August 2021. Many older blogs still say it is needed. Indian visitors are instead asked for an environment fee receipt (next rule).

What we could not verify
  • We did not read the original administration notification itself.
  • Some older pages still say an ILP is required; they appear outdated.

Environment fee receipt and photo ID for restricted-area circuits

Indian tourists pay an environment fee online through the Leh tourist management portal and show the receipt with a photo ID at checkposts for Nubra Valley, Khardung La, Turtuk, Pangong, Tso Moriri and Hanle. The official page confirms that the fee exists and that it includes environment, Red Cross and wildlife components.

Cost: Reported as about ₹450 per person in one 2026 source; another source lists ₹100. The official page we read shows no amount.

How: Pay on the official Leh permit portal (lahdclehpermit.in) or at checkposts. Carry several printed copies of the receipt and ID.

What we could not verify
  • Fee amount is not stated on the official page we could read; third-party sources disagree (₹100 vs ₹450).
  • Exact list of areas covered is not stated on that page.
  • Whether vehicle charges apply separately is not stated.

Protected Area Permit for foreign nationals

Foreign nationals (including OCI and PIO holders per one source) need a Protected Area Permit for inner-line areas, arranged through a registered Leh travel agent. Some Changthang areas, including Hanle, have extra limits for foreigners.

Cost: ₹600 per person reported by one source.

Taxis and self-drive cars rented outside Ladakh cannot do sightseeing

Under a Ladakh taxi union rule, a taxi or self-drive car hired outside Ladakh (Delhi, Manali, Srinagar, Chandigarh) may bring you to Leh but cannot be used for sightseeing to Nubra, Pangong, Tso Moriri or Hanle. You hire a Leh-registered taxi for those trips.

How: Book a local Leh taxi from the union rate list for Nubra, Pangong and Tso Moriri circuits.

What we could not verify
  • Sources describe this as a taxi union resolution that is enforced at checkpoints; we found no government notification making it law.
  • Rates change each season; check the union rate card in Leh.

Bikes rented outside Ladakh cannot be used beyond Leh town

A bike rented in Delhi, Manali or Srinagar can enter Leh but riders are turned back from Nubra, Pangong and Hanle circuits. Riders park it and rent a Leh-registered bike (yellow plate) for those days.

What we could not verify
  • Described as a union rule rather than a government order; enforcement varies.
  • Daily rental rates are set each season.

Your own private vehicle can be driven within Ladakh

Privately owned cars and bikes (white plate) registered in your own name, or reportedly your spouse's or parent's name, are generally allowed to visit Nubra, Pangong and Tso Moriri. Union members at checkpoints may ask for proof of ownership.

Uttarakhand

Confirmed on official pageRegistration

Char Dham yatra registration is compulsory (free)

The Uttarakhand portal says you must register yourself and your vehicle before the journey, download a Yatra Registration Letter with a QR code, and verify yourself at each shrine on arrival. It describes registration as free. Helicopter tickets are sold only through IRCTC.

Cost: Free, according to the official page.

How: Register on registrationandtouristcare.uk.gov.in or the Tourist Care Uttarakhand app. Helpline 1364 (toll free) is listed on the page.

What we could not verify
  • Registration date windows and daily caps change each season.
  • Some third-party sites mention fees; the official page says free. Do not pay agents for registration.

Vehicle registration, Green Card and Trip Card for Char Dham routes

The official transport portal says commercial passenger vehicles (buses, minibuses, taxis) need a Green Card and Trip Card for the Char Dham shrines: ₹400 for up to 10 seats and ₹600 above 10 seats, plus a ₹50 user charge. Private vehicles are asked to register and pay a user charge; no Green Card is mentioned for them.

Cost: Commercial: ₹400 (up to 10 seats) or ₹600 (over 10 seats) plus ₹50 user charge. Private user-charge amount is not stated.

How: Register the vehicle at greencard.uk.gov.in (linked from transport.uk.gov.in).

What we could not verify
  • The private-vehicle user charge amount and Green Card validity are not stated on the page.
  • Some third-party sites say all passenger vehicles including tourists need a Green Card; the official page limits the Green Card to commercial vehicles.

West Bengal

Reported by several sourcesPerson permit

Singalila National Park: entry permit, and a registered guide is required beyond Tumling

Trekking to Sandakphu or Phalut means entering Singalila National Park. You buy an entry permit at the Manebhanjan or Dhotrey forest checkpost and hire a state-registered guide (a porter is optional). If you go only as far as Tumling you do not need the park permit.

How: Pay at the forest checkpost; operators usually arrange the guide.

What we could not verify
  • We did not read a forest department notice.
  • Rules for solo trekkers and for minors are not stated in the sources.

Singalila entry fee, camera fee and vehicle fee

Sources disagree on the numbers. Indian entry: ₹100 to ₹120 per person (per day inside the park in some sources). Foreigners: ₹200 to ₹500. Vehicle: ₹100 or ₹400. Still camera ₹100; video ₹300 to ₹400.

Cost: ₹100 to ₹120 per Indian visitor; ₹100 to ₹400 per vehicle.

What we could not verify
  • Fees differ between sources and years; the forest department's current rate card was not found.
  • Whether the fee is per day or per entry is stated inconsistently.

Jaldapara and Gorumara safari booking rules change often

The forest department restored online booking for elephant safaris at Jaldapara and Gorumara in September 2025 and began online booking at Jaldapara again in October 2026. Jeep or car safaris were booked offline at times. Book only through the forest department's official channels or a registered operator.

What we could not verify
  • Policy has switched between online and offline several times; confirm the current method before you go.
  • Safari fees and vehicle rules are not listed here because we found no reliable current source.
